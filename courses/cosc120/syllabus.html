Course Description

This course surveys computer science and introduces object-oriented programming. A survey of the pivotal fields of computer science, including software engineering, computer networks, programming languages, algorithms, computer architecture, models of computation and operating systems. Students begin to solve simple problems using object-oriented programming. The emphasis is on implementing object-oriented designs. This course is suitable for non-majors who want an overview of computer science and to learn to solve problems with programs. COSC 120 satisfies the Core Curriculum requirement in Mathematics.

More specifically, in this course, we would

  1. Learn to think algorithmically — breaking problems into precise, ordered steps that a computer can execute
  2. Read, write, and reason about programs in Java
  3. Understand how core programming concepts — variables, conditionals, loops, methods, and objects — work and interact
  4. Build a foundation for further study in computer science

Course Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of the course, students will be able to:

  • Interpret class and activity diagrams as demonstrated by creating Java classes based on the diagrams.

  • Identify areas of concentration in computer science as demonstrated by answering related questions on examinations through the semester.

  • Use decision making control structures as demonstrated by writing a Java program which utilizes if else or switch statements.

  • Use looping control structures as demonstrated by writing a Java program which utilizes loops.

  • Replicate object-oriented programs as demonstrated by developing programs from given class diagrams.

  • Implement variables of assorted types as demonstrated by developing programs using simple and complex variables.

  • Explain a basic description of computer science and who a computer scientist is as demonstrated by writing a short paper.

Engaged Learning

Students can expect the Engaged Learning element of this course to deepen their learning by building a creative animation project in Processing (Java) across the second half of the term, supported by scheduled one-on-one progress check-ins with the instructor and a final public showcase of the class’s work.

Engaged Learning is separate from regular homework: credit is earned by completing each milestone (three progress check-ins and the showcase), not by the correctness or polish of the work. See the Programming Project description and the project guide for details.

Methods of Evaluation

Your course grade will be based on homework assignments, quizzes, a midterm exam, a final exam, and a programming project. Most components will emphasize hands-on programming.

  • Homework Assignments - Programming requires consistent practice, so you will complete several assignments throughout the semester. These involve coding on a laptop or desktop computer. You may use your own device, or borrow one (see Course Requirements section above)

  • Quizzes - Programming builds on many small concepts that fit together to form larger systems. To help you stay on track, there will be regular quizzes focused on these building blocks.

  • Midterm Exam and Final Exam - These exams will assess your cumulative knowledge and problem-solving skills.

  • Programming Project - A larger-scale, open-ended project: a creative animation or interactive sketch built in Processing (Java). You choose your own concept. It is graded on how well it uses the course’s building blocks — variables, conditionals, methods, loops, arrays, and a class of your own — not on artistic merit. The project is developed over the second half of the term through the Engaged Learning check-ins and presented at the end-of-term showcase.

Course Attendance Policy

Attendance is not mandatory, and thus absence from class is not penalized directly, but you will lose out in other ways. Class attendance is the #1 indicator of academic success. OS3 emphatically encourages all students to Go To Class! Class attendance is important for numerous reasons:

  • Students tend to remember info more when they attend class.
  • Your presence in class shows your instructor that you care about the course and it is important to you. If your grade is borderline at the end of the semester, the instructor may give you the benefit of the doubt because you regularly attended class.
  • You/your families are paying to be in class. In-state residents who miss a class are wasting over $56 per class. Out-of-state students who miss a class are throwing away $110 per class!
  • Your instructor will likely explain material differently/better than you can learn it yourself, and can answer questions more directly.
  • Going to class helps you build community and relationships with peers and your instructors.
  • By not going to class, you may miss a pop quiz, participation points, extra credit opportunities, important info, or in-class assignments that can impact your grade.

To the Point establishes the baseline attendance policy for SMCM courses. 

Please note: Per the SMCM policy, there is no distinction between “excused” or “unexcused” absences; an absence is an absence.

Late Work and Missed Assignment Policy

Assignments are due at 11:59 PM Eastern Time (UTC – 4) on the posted due date, with a one-hour grace period. Submissions received after that time will be subject to a late penalty of 10% of the maximum possible points per additional day past the deadline. The minimum grade on any assignment is zero; late penalties will not reduce a score below this.

Because the penalty accumulates continuously, assignments submitted several days late will earn little or no credit. Please plan accordingly and submit on time.

This policy is designed to ensure fairness and to allow me to return graded work to the class within 10 days of the due date. General requests for deadline extension requests will not be entertained after the scores for the assessment item has been posted.

Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence in the Classroom 

Generative artificial intelligence (AI) (tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) is a fast-developing tool that greatly impacts the learning process. As with many things, there are times when using these tools is appropriate and when it is not.  For this course, I encourage you to use the generative AI tools at your disposal for learning and practice, but not for homework assignments and quizzes. However it is your responsibility to properly evaluate the information created through generative AI. We will discuss how to do this in class, and you should contact me with any questions that you may have if you encounter a situation where it is unclear how to do use AI. Failure to disclose the use generative AI tools in assignments and quizzes may be considered plagiarism and will be handled via the SMCM Academic Misconduct policies.
